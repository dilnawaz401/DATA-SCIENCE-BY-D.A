2. Text Preprocessing

Before giving text to a Machine Learning model, we need to clean and convert it.

The lecture starts with examples such as:

"The food is good"
"The food is not good"
"Pizza is amazing"

The important idea is:

Computer cannot directly understand words like humans, so text must be converted into numerical representation.

3. One-Hot Encoding

Each word is represented using 0 and 1.

Suppose vocabulary is:

food
good
pizza
amazing

Then a word can be represented like:

food    → [1,0,0,0]
good    → [0,1,0,0]
pizza   → [0,0,1,0]
amazing → [0,0,0,1]
Problem

If vocabulary contains 10,000 words, every word gets a vector of size 10,000.

This creates a sparse matrix.

Problems with One-Hot Encoding
Very large vectors
Sparse matrix
Does not capture semantic meaning
Vocabulary size determines vector size
4. Bag of Words — BoW

Bag of Words represents a sentence/document using the frequency/count of words.

Example:

S1 = "good boy"
S2 = "good girl"
S3 = "bad boy"

Vocabulary:

good, boy, girl, bad

Then we count how many times each word appears.

The important thing is:

BoW cares about word occurrence/frequency, not word order.

For example:

"Dog bites man"
"Man bites dog"

BoW may give similar representations because it ignores word order.

5. N-Grams

N-Gram means looking at groups of N consecutive words.

Unigram

One word:

I love NLP

→ I
→ love
→ NLP
Bigram

Two words:

I love
love NLP
Trigram

Three words:

I love NLP
Formula/idea
Unigram → 1 word
Bigram  → 2 words
Trigram → 3 words

N-Grams help capture some word-order information, which basic Bag of Words cannot.

6. TF-IDF

TF-IDF = Term Frequency × Inverse Document Frequency

It tells us how important a word is in a document.

TF — Term Frequency

How frequently a word appears in a document.

TF =
Number of times word appears
----------------------------
Total number of words
IDF — Inverse Document Frequency

It gives less importance to words that appear in many documents.

Simple idea:

Common word everywhere
        ↓
    Low importance

Rare/important word
        ↓
    High importance
TF-IDF
TF-IDF = TF × IDF
Easy example

Words like:

the
is
a

appear in many documents, so they generally receive lower importance.

A specific word appearing mainly in one document can receive higher importance.

7. Word Embedding

This is an important part of the lecture.

Instead of representing a word using huge sparse vectors, we represent it using a dense numerical vector.

Word
 ↓
Word Embedding
 ↓
Numerical Vector

Example:

King → [0.25, 0.71, 0.32, ...]
Queen → [0.24, 0.69, 0.35, ...]

The vectors capture semantic relationships between words.

The PDF explains word embedding as representing words with real-valued vectors where nearby vectors are expected to have similar meanings.

8. Word2Vec ⭐

Word2Vec is one of the main topics in the PDF.

It is a technique for learning word embeddings.

The basic idea:

Words appearing in similar contexts tend to have similar meanings.

Example:

The king rules the kingdom.
The queen rules the kingdom.

The model learns relationships between words from their contexts.

9. Word2Vec — Two Methods

There are two major approaches:

1. CBOW

CBOW = Continuous Bag of Words

It uses surrounding/context words to predict the target word.

Context Words
     ↓
   CBOW
     ↓
Target Word

Example:

"The cat is ___ on the mat"

Context → The, cat, is, on, the, mat
                     ↓
                   CBOW
                     ↓
                  sleeping
2. Skip-Gram

Skip-Gram does the opposite.

It uses a target word to predict surrounding/context words.

Target Word
     ↓
 Skip-Gram
     ↓
Context Words

Example:

Target → cat
   ↓
Skip-Gram
   ↓
the, is, on, mat
🧠 Easy Memory

CBOW → Context → Word

Skip-Gram → Word → Context

10. Word2Vec Semantic Relationship

The PDF also demonstrates the famous relationship:

King - Man + Woman ≈ Queen

The idea is that Word2Vec vectors can capture relationships between words.

Similarly:

King → Male concept
Queen → Female concept

The important point is semantic information is captured in the vector representation.

11. Cosine Similarity

To compare word vectors, the lecture introduces Cosine Similarity.

It measures how similar two vectors are based on their direction.

Simple idea:

Vectors point in similar direction
          ↓
     High similarity

Vectors point in different directions
          ↓
     Low similarity

So if:

King vector
Queen vector

are close in vector space, their cosine similarity can be high.

12. Word2Vec Advantages

According to the lecture, Word2Vec improves over simple representations because it:

Captures semantic information
Produces dense vectors
Has a fixed embedding size
Avoids the huge sparse representation problem
Helps represent relationships between words
